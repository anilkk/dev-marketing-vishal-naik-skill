# Reference card

One page. Every framework in the skill. Frameworks by Vishal Naik, [source playbook](https://sharebird.com/h/product-marketing/playbooks/how-docusign-increased-their-developer-marketing-conversion-rates-by-50).

## The axiom

> Developers are not one person. Know the developer, know the decision maker, know the end user, connect all three.

## The 7 principles of developer marketing

| # | Principle | Test |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Know the user, connect to the product | Can you name what this developer is trying to build? |
| 2 | Polished messaging, immediate action | Is there anything to try today, or only a promise? |
| 3 | Cater to different developer segments | Does the plan read differently for an ML engineer and a full stack developer? |
| 4 | Provide tangible value | Docs, codelabs, demos, sandbox, in their language and IDE? |
| 5 | Understand paths of usage | Have you tailored support per platform and integration? |
| 6 | Time launches with drip delivery | Is anticipation built before the day one slate lands? |
| 7 | Focus on relevant channels | Is there a defined route to market, or just a blog post? |

## The 4 personas

| Persona | Situation | What they need first |
|---|---|---|
| SMB developer | Handling many responsibilities alone | Self serve path, no gate, fast quickstart |
| Enterprise developer | Specialized team inside an IT system | Compliance, security, architecture answers |
| "Doer" developer | Focused on building applications | Working code in their language, today |
| ISV partner | Evaluating systems to build for end clients | Partner terms, multi tenant patterns, roadmap confidence |

## The 5 step process

| Step | Name | Output | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Develop hypothesis | A stated belief about who is actually showing up | Analytical |
| 2 | Collect data | Opportunity quantified in a pitch deck | Analytical |
| 3 | Map the journey | Cross department action plan | Political |
| 4 | Get buy in | Buy in deck presented, stakeholders aligned | Political |
| 5 | Translate to project plans | Individual plans with owners and dates | Political |

Most plans die between step 2 and step 4.

## Deck structures

**Opportunity deck.** Lead with the opportunity and the number. Get to the point fast. Tailor to the audience in the room. Show the discrepancy, the data, the size of the underserved segment, the revenue implication.

**Buy in deck.** The journey, the plan, and what each team owns. Name every stakeholder's win.

**Stakeholders to plan for:** product management, developer relations, technical content writers, UX, core product marketing, engineering, corporate marketing covering web, email and demand generation, COO, CTO, sales, solutions engineering.

**Prioritization matrix.** Define the funnel components, list opportunities, let stakeholder teams weigh in democratically, then commit to the annual plan.

## Launch slate, day one

A launch is not ready until all of these ship together:

- [ ] Blog content
- [ ] Updated technical documentation
- [ ] Sample documentation and code samples
- [ ] Codelabs, per language where relevant
- [ ] Webinar or demo
- [ ] Updated developer center and resources
- [ ] Drip communications sent before launch day

## Metrics stack

**Funnel health:** email click through rate, follower counts, event attendance.

**Business impact:**

- Developer trial sign ups
- Trial to live integration conversion rate
- Live integrations in production
- Speed to first integration
- Go live success rate
- Integrations per customer
- Endpoint call volume

**Three reporting dimensions:** momentum in the platform, quantified end user value, impact to the business.

## Channels, ranked by his experience

Email first. It was the number one driver of action at DocuSign, consistent across web and mobile customers. Then YouTube, Stack Overflow, Twitch, Reddit. Then route to market partnerships that put the product inside tools developers already work in.

## Sales enablement rule

Arm sales at the 101 level so they know enough to be dangerous, meaning they can hold a basic technical conversation and know which expert to bring in next. Skip the 400 level content they will never use.
